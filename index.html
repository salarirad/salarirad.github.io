Mostafa Salari Rad

I'm a behavioral scientist interested in how people make decisions, cooperate, make sense of others, and support policies, across cultures and identities. I also build the tools to measure this in the real world.

I trained as a software engineer, worked as a market research consultant while getting my MBA, then earned a PhD in psychology. Since then, I've taught at Princeton and The New School, and I lead a Templeton-funded research program using Ramadan as a natural experiment in decision-making and cooperation.

I'm an Editorial Fellow at the Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. I review research for journals and grant-makers, and advise on research design and data analysis.

Outside work, I like films, fiction, photography, and hanging out with Zorba.

Research

Decisions, cooperation, and social cognition across cultures — papers and ongoing projects.

My research moves from the self to society: how people make decisions and pursue their goals, and how they perceive and categorize others. I study how everyday beliefs and practices, like folk theories and rituals, shape cooperation, relationships, and policy views, and how much of it all is cultural.

Ramadan as a natural experiment

Each year, hundreds of millions of people fast from dawn to sunset for a month. I lead a research program that uses Ramadan to study self-regulation, cognition, and cooperation outside the lab, supported by a $499,887 grant from the Templeton World Charity Foundation.

Decisions

    Religion, Brain & Behavior, 2025

    Over the month, fasters improved more in inhibitory control than non-fasters, especially those high in trait self-control.

  • From self-deprivation to cooperation: How Ramadan fasting influences risk-aversion and decisions in resource dilemmas

    Current Research in Ecological and Social Psychology, 2023 · PDF

    Fasting was linked to more risk-averse and more cooperative choices in anonymous resource dilemmas.

  • Ramadan fasting as a goal-pursuit: Why people fast, and how motives affect their experience

    Motivation Science, 2023

    Why people fast shapes how hard the fast feels.

  • Temporary self-deprivation can impair cognitive control: Evidence from the Ramadan fast

    Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2022. Rad et al.

    Food reminders made Ramadan fasters slower and less accurate on an unrelated control task, so chosen deprivation still taxes attention.

  • Loss of control is not necessary to induce behavioral consequences of deprivation: The case of religious fasting during Ramadan

    Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 2017. With Ginges

    Self-imposed deprivation produces risk and loss aversion even when people are in control, which challenges scarcity-only accounts.

Culture, identity and politics

  • Misperceptions in protest affect and action among resident and diaspora activists: Online versus on-the-ground in the 2009–2012 Iranian post-election protests

    Journal of Social and Political Psychology, 2026 · PDF

    Resident and diaspora activists from Iran's 2009 protests experienced them similarly, yet each misread the other: diaspora members imagined protests felt worse on the ground, and residents saw diaspora activists as focused on mechanics rather than purpose.

  • Folk theories of gender and anti-transgender attitudes: Gender differences and policy preferences

    PLoS ONE, 2019. Rad et al. · PDF

Tools

Cut, Lens, and Jamasp: open tools for designing, running, and collecting behavioral and wearable studies.

I build open tools that take behavioral research out of the lab: design and run studies in the browser, and collect real-world data from wearables.

Jamasp

Current

Recruits people who already own wearables and collects their heart rate, sleep, and activity data, from consent through to an analysis-ready database. It pulls data both retroactively and going forward, for whatever period a study needs, and now connects through the Google Health API, beyond Fitbit.

Researchers can query their data in plain language through a built-in chat assistant powered by Gemini.

In a 65-day pilot with 41 Fitbit owners recruited through Prolific, Jamasp kept data completeness high across all three streams.

jamasp.app Code and data from the pilot

Lens

Co-creator, 2018–present

Mix surveys with interactive tasks, from risk and inhibition to memory and economic games, in one study file that runs in any browser, in any language. Lens 2.0 also runs live multiplayer games, matching participants in real time inside a survey. Funded by The New School, APS, and the Templeton World Charity Foundation, and used across my Ramadan studies.

Try the tasks Study builder Source on GitHub

Cut

2014–2018, Lens's predecessor

A full experiment platform with real-time multiplayer games: participants were matched live for ultimatum and dictator games, with configurable bots stepping in when no partner was available. It also included a visual study designer, Mechanical Turk management, and multilingual support. It ran our early Ramadan studies and a 2016 election study. Its live games now run in Lens.
